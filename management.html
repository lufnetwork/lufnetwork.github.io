I. Framework for Program Delivery

The LUF NETWORK (Local Urban Forum Network) is managed through a centralized, high-capacity Secretariat Structure that strategically anchors a specialized core officer layer: a Programme Coordinator, Finance and Administration Manager, Research and Advocacy Officer, Monitoring and Evaluation Officer, Information and Communications Officer, Program Accountant, Program Secretary, and vital Support Staff.

"The Secretariat operates as the definitive executive decision-making body for the programme. It delivers overarching strategic oversight alongside rigorous day-to-day managerial mechanics, while actively auditing and monitoring all implementing partner tracks inside informal zones."

In discharging its executive mandate, the Secretariat draws intensive technical advisory streams and capacity-building solutions from a regularized, multi-agency roster: the Technical Advisory Group (TAG) and the Capacity Building Team (CBT) respectively. The Secretariat is fully responsible for processing capacity bottlenecks at all tiers of field execution and dynamically matching those demands with specialized experts available through the TAG and CBT networks. TAG serves as the primary advisory intelligence body to the Secretariat and surrounding structural organs.

Implementing Partners

Selected Civil Society Organizations (CSOs) responsible for field deployment of demonstration projects that present best-practice models targeted at urban poor groups within informal settlements.

Community Working Groups

The grassroots presence of LUF NETWORK is coordinated via Community Sector Working Groups (CSWGs). Aligned with project implementation teams, they draw members from a broad community cross-section.

Target Beneficiaries

The ultimate beneficiaries of our structural interventions are the urban poor, inclusive of women, men, youth, children, persons with disabilities, and highly vulnerable groups resident in informal zones.

II & IV. Project Planning Tools & Processes

LUF NETWORK utilizes strict participatory planning mechanisms to secure maximum local context and bottom-up transparency. Participatory Community Appraisal (PCA) models and Community Action Plans (CAPs) serve as the operational platforms through which local informal populations directly articulate their baseline needs.
