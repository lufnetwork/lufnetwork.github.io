Our National Mandate

The Local Urban Forum Network (LUF NETWORK) operates across 14 towns in Kenya—including Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret, and Vihiga—with our headquarters centered in Kibra and Kariobangi, Nairobi.

Regional Strategic Stations

Our SITE Offices are strategically placed to serve rural and urban municipalities within their jurisdictions. We maintain regional branches located per administrative boundary to ensure each location benefits immensely from the localized station therein.

Our Founding Journey & Evolution

While LUF NETWORK traces its initial community roots back to 2003, our specialized institutional framework was formally accelerated in 2014. Operating as a rights-based Public Affairs Forum and a civil society think-tank, the organization grew out of an urgent necessity to address the severe socio-economic impact of localized health gaps and systemic vulnerabilities.

Since 2014, we have transitioned from basic rapport building and Participatory Community Appraisals (PCAs) to advanced problem analysis, village-level work plans, and formalized institutional implementation. Our journey is defined by a reflective, self-critical approach that ensures our strategies are rapidly informed by the communities we serve.

Strategic Development Matrix

2014 – 2017: Capacity Alignment

Establishing the SITE offices in Kibra and Kariobangi. Focused on staff selection, recruitment of local and expatriate consultants, and initial multi-stakeholder linkage building.

2018 – 2021: Systemization & Synergy

Designing rigorous Monitoring & Evaluation systems. Moving from rapid health responses to long-term sustainable management of population-level health trends and rights advocacy.

2022 – 2026: Multi-Pillar Climate Resilience

Launching the 9-Pillar model. Integrating NOAA-sponsored climate applications and IGAD (ICPAC) mitigation strategies into our urban resilience and maternal-mental health frameworks.

Climate Resilience & Mitigation

In response to the escalating global climate crisis, LUF NETWORK has established a dedicated track for the development of mitigation strategies for slow and rapid weather-related disasters. We operate in synergy with international frameworks, including NOAA-sponsored Pilot Climate Applications Projects through the IGAD Climate Prediction and Application Centre (ICPAC).

Strategic Goal: Increasing Community Resilience

Our primary objective is to strengthen the resilience of resource-poor communities against the vagaries of weather, low environmental quality, and climate-induced socio-economic shocks.

  • 🌱 Awareness: Raising community consciousness regarding the systemic burdens imposed by environmental degradation and extreme weather.
  • 🛠️ Technology: Promoting the application of appropriate green technologies and localized interventions to lessen environmental burdens.
  • 📈 Capacity: Strengthening the technical and management skills of local communities to address climate damages and infrastructure vulnerabilities.

Advancing Inclusive Devolution

In complement to Kenya’s process of devolution, LUF NETWORK believes that one of our core duties is to ensure that local dialogue is truly inclusive. We act as a catalyst to ensure that previously marginalized groups—specifically the poor, women, youth, and people with neurological or psychiatric vulnerabilities—receive a fair chance to be heard and to influence the decisions that shape their urban environments.

The Forums Agenda

  • • Establishing structured, rights-based development dialogue across 14 urban centers in Kenya.
  • • Facilitating multi-stakeholder engagement between civil society, the private sector, and government bodies.
  • • Securing institutional visibility for informal neighborhood networks to enable stable, professional partnerships.
  • • Monitoring compliance with the Urban Areas and Cities Act to protect the fundamental rights of all urban citizens.

Program Methodology: The 9 Pillars

Our impact is delivered through a specialized 9-pillar framework designed to address the multifaceted challenges of urban informal environments. Each pillar operates as a dedicated coordination track to ensure direct resource management and systemic resilience.

1. Community Support Direct relief, food security, and emergency response loops.
2. Education & Skills Literacy nodes and professional vocational training.
3. Advocacy & Rights Protecting legal equality and urban civic voices.
4. Health & Wellness Specialized psychiatric and maternal-mental safety loops.
5. Sustainability Ecological conservation and circular resource initiatives.
6. Economic Empowerment Micro-enterprise support and financial independence.
7. Urban Resilience Disaster preparedness and climate adaptation matrices.
8. Social Equity Inclusive protection for marginalized urban groups.
9. Institutional Synergy Bridging grassroots data with national policy frameworks.
