Our National Mandate

The Local Urban Forum Network (LUF NETWORK) was established through the support of Multi-stakeholder Citizens Forums across 14 towns in Kenya—including Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, Eldoret, and Vihiga, with Nairobi serving as our headquarters.

The Foundation of Our Dialogue

Our purpose is to create a secure space for rights-based development dialogue. While we are pioneered in civil society, our dialogue is inclusively oriented with the ambition to engage the Government and the Private Sector in local discourse.

⚖️ A defining principle of our work is the Urban Areas and Cities ACT of 2011 (UACA, 2011).

Our Founding Journey

The LUF NETWORK stems from a deep-rooted commitment to social justice, sustainable urban planning, and direct public resource management. We arose out of a vital community necessity to establish an organized structure capable of shielding vulnerable populations within dense informal environments.

Our journey began when grassroots community mobilizers, public health advocates, and local leaders witnessed firsthand the severe gaps in relief delivery, primary care access, and youth livelihood protections inside congested urban sectors. Informal networks lacked the structural visibility needed to secure stable, professional partnerships with major institutional bodies.

By mobilizing neighborhood networks block by block, LUF NETWORK transformed from an informal group into a specialized multi-pillar coordination framework. We bridge the gap between high-level development policy and on-the-ground survival, giving local communities a trusted mechanism to manage resource flows transparently.

Advancing Inclusive Devolution

In complement to Kenya’s process of devolution, LUF NETWORK believes that one of our core duties is to ensure that local dialogue is truly inclusive. We act as a catalyst to ensure that previously marginalized groups—specifically the poor, women, youth, and people with neurological or psychiatric vulnerabilities—receive a fair chance to be heard and to influence the decisions that shape their urban lives.

The Forums Agenda

  • • Establishing structured, rights-based development dialogue across 14 urban centers.
  • • Facilitating multi-stakeholder engagement between civil society, government, and the private sector.
  • • Securing institutional visibility for informal neighborhood networks to enable professional partnerships.
  • • Monitoring compliance with the Urban Areas and Cities Act to protect the fundamental rights of urban citizens.

Program Methodology: The 9 Pillars

Our impact is delivered through a specialized 9-pillar framework designed to address the multifaceted challenges of urban informal environments. Each pillar operates as a dedicated coordination track to ensure direct resource management and systemic resilience.

1. Community Support Direct relief and emergency response loops.
2. Education & Skills Literacy and vocational training nodes.
3. Advocacy & Rights Protecting legal equality and urban voices.
4. Health & Wellness Specialized psychiatric and maternal safety loops.
5. Sustainability Ecological conservation and green initiatives.
6. Economic Empowerment Micro-enterprise and mentorship programs.
7. Urban Resilience Disaster preparedness and flood management.
8. Social Equity Inclusive protection for marginalized groups.
9. Institutional Synergy Bridging grassroots data with ministerial policy.
